Your career map

A path built one step at a time.

There isn’t just one route into systems work. Build hands-on experience, deepen your technical skills, and follow the direction that interests you.

Entry level 01 / START

IT Support Technician

Also known as Help Desk Technician

Help people use technology by diagnosing everyday hardware, software, account, and connectivity issues.

What you’ll do
  • Triage support requests and document solutions
  • Set up devices, accounts, and approved software
  • Resolve common desktop and network problems
Build toward: customer service, troubleshooting, Windows or Linux basics, and networking fundamentals.
Intermediate 02 / GROW

System Administrator

A natural next step from frontline support

Operate and maintain the servers, services, user accounts, and infrastructure that an organization relies on.

What you’ll do
  • Manage servers, access, updates, and backups
  • Monitor service health and investigate incidents
  • Automate repeatable maintenance tasks
Build toward: deeper OS administration, scripting, identity management, security, and cloud basics.
Advanced 03 / LEAD

Systems Engineer

Or Senior Administrator / Infrastructure Manager

Design, improve, and guide the systems and infrastructure behind complex, organization-wide services.

What you’ll do
  • Plan scalable, resilient infrastructure changes
  • Lead complex incidents and technical projects
  • Set standards and mentor other IT professionals
Build toward: architecture, cloud and automation, risk management, communication, and technical leadership.

Skills & qualifications

A strong foundation for solving the next problem.

You don’t have to master everything on day one. These fundamentals make you more effective at each career stage.

01

Operating systems

Learn Windows and Linux accounts, permissions, updates, services, and logs to manage the environments organizations run.

02

Networking

Understand IP addressing, DNS, DHCP, routing, and firewalls so you can trace how devices and services connect.

03

Troubleshooting

Use a calm, repeatable process to gather evidence, identify root causes, restore service, and document what happened.

04

Security fundamentals

Apply least privilege, patching, multi-factor authentication, and safe backup practices to reduce avoidable risk.

05

Scripting & automation

Use PowerShell or Bash to standardize routine tasks, reduce manual errors, and make repeatable work faster.

Job roles & responsibilities

Different roles. Different perspectives.

Support keeps users moving; administrators keep services healthy; engineers plan how those services evolve.

Role Primary focus Typical responsibilities Scope
IT Support Technician Helping individual users Answer requests, troubleshoot workstations, set up accounts and devices, and escalate complex issues. Day-to-day user and endpoint support
System Administrator Keeping IT services reliable Maintain servers and access, monitor services, apply updates, manage backups, and respond to incidents. Shared systems across teams
Systems Engineer Designing what comes next Architect infrastructure, plan migrations, improve automation and resilience, and guide technical projects. Organization-wide systems and future growth
